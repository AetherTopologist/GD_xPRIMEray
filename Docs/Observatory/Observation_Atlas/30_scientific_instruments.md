# 30 Scientific Instruments

## Scope

Scientific instruments couple a phenomenon, interaction region, detector, calibration process, and reported measurement. Atlas examples include CPC and particle counters, SEM, TEM, STM, AFM, spectrometers, cameras, and detector arrays.

Entries in this family should follow the shared [Observer Grammar](observer_grammar.md).

## Instrument Families

| Family | Observation emphasis |
|---|---|
| Condensation particle counter and particle counter | Event or population detection under declared sampling and calibration conditions |
| SEM and TEM | Electron-beam interaction and detector-derived image or analytical channels |
| STM and AFM | Scanned probe response over a surface under declared feedback and reconstruction rules |
| Spectrometer | Signal distribution over wavelength, frequency, energy, mass, or another calibrated axis |
| Camera and detector array | Spatially sampled response with declared spectral, temporal, and transfer characteristics |

## Atlas Recording Pattern

Each instrument entry should identify stimulus, interaction mechanism, detector, calibration, units, dynamic range, reconstruction, artifacts, and uncertainty. Similar-looking outputs from different instruments remain distinct measurement channels unless a documented transform establishes a comparison basis.

Project Glowing Heart provides the emerging protocol layer for describing fixtures, observers, snapshots, measurements, artifacts, and future comparisons. Its v1.9 measurement vocabulary is an early computational analogue, not an instrument-validation result.

## Claim Boundary

No parity claim.
No physics validation claim.
No claim that artistic or speculative visualizations are scientific proof.
